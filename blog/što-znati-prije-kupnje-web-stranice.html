Guide

What to know before
buying a website

A guide for small businesses — what to look for, what to watch out for, and how to make the right decision.

August 27, 2026 · 6 min read

A website isn't a luxury — it's a business tool. But if you don't know what to look for, it's easy to spend too much for too little. This guide will help you make an informed decision.

1. Define the goal of the website

Before thinking about design or price, answer the question: what should the website achieve?

  • Get inquiries — people find you, send a message, call
  • Sell products — web shop with cart and payments
  • Inform — restaurant, apartment, business — basic info + contact
  • Build trust — portfolio, references, testimonials

The goal determines everything else — structure, design, price.

2. Know what's included in the price

The price of a website isn't just "design." Here's what you get:

  • Design — look, layout, colors, typography
  • Development — code, responsive adaptation, features
  • Content — copy, photos, SEO optimization
  • Hosting + domain — annual cost for keeping the site online
  • Maintenance — security updates, backup, support

Ask what's included and what's charged extra.

3. Template vs. custom design

There are two approaches:

Template (shablona) — cheaper, faster, but limited. You get a ready-made design with minor customization. Problem: same as hundreds of other websites.

Custom design — more expensive, but unique. The site is built for your brand, your customers, your goals. Takes longer, converts better.

For small businesses, the best compromise is often: semi-custom design — built on a proven structure, but with your brand and needs.

4. Mobile first

Over 60% of traffic comes from mobile. If the site doesn't work well on mobile, you're losing most potential customers.

Ask: "How will it look on mobile?" — and request a demo on a phone before you pay.

5. SEO isn't optional — it's the foundation

Without SEO, your website is like a billboard in a forest. Nobody sees it.

Essentials you need from day one:

  • Proper headings (H1, H2, H3)
  • Page descriptions (meta description)
  • Alt tags on images
  • Google Business profile
  • Fast loading (under 3 seconds)

6. Maintenance isn't a cost — it's an investment

A website isn't "build and forget." Security updates, backup, SSL certificate — all of this needs regular attention. Without maintenance, the site becomes vulnerable and slow.

Your business deserves a website that works, every day.
